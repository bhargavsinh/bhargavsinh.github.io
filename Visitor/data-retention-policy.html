Revised Data Retention Policy (As per IT Act & DPDP Act 2023) - Version 2.1

Effective Date: 08 November 2026 · Version: 2.1

1. Introduction & Purpose

This Policy describes retention, archival, and deletion of personal data consistent with purpose limitation and storage limitation under the DPDP Act, 2023, and with security and evidence needs under the IT Act, 2000 and general law.

2. Scope (What is covered)

Form submissions, email correspondence, grievance files, and technical logs under the Author’s practical control. Hosting-provider logs controlled solely by GitHub/CDN are subject to those providers’ retention schedules.

3. Detailed Clauses with current laws reference

3.1 Form and email data. Retained for as long as needed to respond to the request, complete an appointment discussion, maintain a record of consent or transaction context, or comply with law. Thereafter deleted or archived with restricted access.

3.2 Grievance records. Retained for a period sufficient to demonstrate compliance with IT Rules timelines and to defend legal claims, typically not less than the limitation period relevant to the subject-matter, unless a longer hold is required.

3.3 Hosting logs. Controlled by GitHub / CDN retention practices. The Author does not independently warehouse full traffic logs beyond what the host provides in standard tools.

3.4 Client-side storage. Theme and preference keys remain until cleared by the User or by Site logic.

3.5 Legal hold. Data may be retained longer where reasonably necessary for disputes, investigations, regulatory inquiries, or statutory obligations.

3.6 Erasure requests. Data principals may request erasure of personal data they provided, subject to exceptions under the DPDP Act (e.g., legal claims, compliance). Public historical content that is not your personal data is not subject to personal erasure demands.

3.7 Disposal. When deletion is due, the Author will use reasonable means to delete or irreversibly anonymise data under the Author’s control.

4. User Rights & Responsibilities

You may request deletion of your submitted personal data via the Grievance Officer. You must not demand erasure of public historical or literary content that does not constitute your personal data. Provide enough information to locate the record.

5. Content Protection Measures

The Author maintains a Zero Tolerance posture toward unauthorised copying, scraping, republication, commercial exploitation, and circumvention of technical protection measures. The following layered deterrence measures (soft DRM — not cryptographic DRM) are deployed on protected pages:

  • CSS / UI controls: user-select: none and related rules on protected views to restrict casual text selection outside expressly permitted fields.
  • Anti-copy / anti-cut / anti-paste: Client-side interception of copy, cut, paste, and context-menu actions on protected content (inputs, search, and [data-allow-copy] zones excepted).
  • Keyboard shortcut deterrence: Blocking of common shortcuts (including Ctrl/Cmd + C, X, A, S, U, P) outside editable fields.
  • Image protection: Drag-disable, protected image classes, and restricted touch callouts on media.
  • Watermark / print notice: Print and certain views may carry ownership notices identifying Bhargavsinh Sisodiya and the canonical site URL.
  • Soft DRM deterrence: Layered client-side controls intended to deter casual infringement. Circumvention for the purpose of infringement is Strictly Prohibited and may attract civil and criminal consequences under the Copyright Act, 1957 and the Information Technology Act, 2000, as applicable.

These measures are deterrents. They do not waive any statutory right of the Author. Fair dealing under the Copyright Act, 1957 remains subject to law and does not authorise wholesale reproduction, redistribution, or commercial use.

6. Penalties for Violation

Zero Tolerance applies to wilful infringement, data misuse, security circumvention, spam, and abusive conduct. Without prejudice to other remedies available in law or equity:

  • Immediate suspension, restriction, or blocking of access where technically feasible;
  • Civil claims for injunction, damages, delivery-up, and account of profits under the Copyright Act, 1957 and the Indian Contract Act, 1872;
  • Complaints and proceedings under the Information Technology Act, 2000 (including provisions relating to computer-related offences, damage to computer systems, and breach of protected systems, as applicable to the facts);
  • Reporting to competent law-enforcement or regulatory authorities where required or appropriate under Indian law.

Failure by the Author to enforce any provision in a particular instance shall not constitute a waiver of rights in that or any other instance. Severability: if any clause is held unenforceable, the remainder continues in force.

7. Contact for Grievance Officer

In accordance with Rule 3(2) of the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021, and for data-principal requests under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, the following officer is designated for this digital estate:

  • Name: Bhargavsinh Sisodiya
  • Designation: Grievance Officer / Data Protection Contact
  • Email: bhargavsinh.bca@gmail.com
  • Website: https://bhargavsinh.github.io/
  • Acknowledgement: within fifteen (15) days of receipt of a complete written complaint.
  • Resolution endeavour: within a reasonable period, ordinarily not exceeding thirty (30) days, subject to complexity and verification needs.

How to file: Send an email with subject line identifying the policy (e.g., “Grievance — Privacy Policy”), your full name, contact details, description of the issue, relevant URLs, and relief sought. Incomplete complaints may be returned for particulars. Frivolous, vexatious, or abusive complaints may be closed with recorded reasons and, where warranted, reported under applicable law.

This mechanism does not limit statutory remedies before competent authorities or courts under Indian law.
