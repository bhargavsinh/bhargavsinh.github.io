Revised Privacy Policy (As per IT Act & DPDP Act 2023) - Version 2.1

Effective Date: 08 November 2026 · Controller / Data Fiduciary (limited purpose): Bhargavsinh Sisodiya · Prior version: 2.0

1. Introduction & Purpose

This Privacy Policy explains how personal data is collected, processed, stored, shared, retained, and deleted in connection with the digital estate operated at https://bhargavsinh.github.io/ (the “Site”), including its portals (Visitor, HR Recruiter, Book & Publications) and related forms.

It is framed to align, to the extent applicable to a personal, primarily non-commercial digital portfolio hosted on static infrastructure, with:

  • the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (“DPDP Act”);
  • the Information Technology Act, 2000 (“IT Act”);
  • the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021 (“IT Rules, 2021”); and
  • related principles of fair processing under Indian law.

The Site is operated by Bhargavsinh Sisodiya (the “Author”). For the limited purposes of this Policy, the Author acts as the person determining the purpose and means of processing of personal data submitted through the Site. Strictly Prohibited: any attempt to harvest, reverse-engineer, scrape, or misuse personal or technical data beyond the purposes stated herein.

By using the Site or submitting information, you acknowledge that you have read this Policy. Where consent is the basis of processing, submission of a form or email constitutes clear affirmative action for the stated purpose.

2. Scope (What is covered)

This Policy applies to:

  • visitors and users of the Site and its sub-portals;
  • persons who submit appointment/contact forms or send email to the Author;
  • technical and security logs generated by hosting and content-delivery infrastructure; and
  • grievance and correspondence records maintained for legal compliance.

This Policy does not convert static historical, genealogical, literary, or archival material concerning deceased persons into “personal data” of living users merely by display. Public-domain or author-compiled historical content is governed primarily by the Copyright Policy and Content Editorial Policy.

3. Detailed Clauses with current laws reference

3.1 Categories of data.

  • Identity & contact data you voluntarily provide (name, email address, phone number, message content, preferred appointment details) via Formspree-powered forms or direct email.
  • Technical data that may be processed by GitHub Pages and associated CDN networks (truncated or full IP addresses, user-agent strings, device/browser metadata, access timestamps, referrer) for delivery, security, DDoS mitigation, and abuse detection.
  • Functional client storage such as localStorage/sessionStorage for UI preferences (theme, font scale, intro state, last portal). These are not advertising trackers.
  • Grievance records created when you lodge a complaint under the IT Rules, 2021 mechanism.

3.2 Purpose limitation (DPDP Act). Personal data is processed only for: responding to enquiries and appointment requests; providing information about publications and projects; security and fraud/abuse prevention; grievance redressal; and compliance with applicable law. Strictly Prohibited: sale, rental, barter, or monetisation of personal data; building advertising profiles by the Author; using submitted data for unrelated marketing lists.

3.3 Lawful basis. Processing is based on (a) consent evidenced by voluntary form or email submission for a stated purpose; and/or (b) legitimate uses consistent with the DPDP Act and the Author’s security obligations under the IT Act, including prevention of unauthorised access and abuse.

3.4 Processors and third parties. Formspree (form delivery) and GitHub (static hosting/CDN) act as infrastructure or processors under their respective terms and privacy notices. The Author does not control their security architectures. Users must not submit government identity numbers, passwords, financial credentials, or health data through public forms.

3.5 Security. Reasonable security practices appropriate to a static personal site are maintained (HTTPS delivery via the host, access control to repositories, content-security considerations). No system is immune from risk. Unauthorised access, scanning, or intrusion attempts may attract liability under the IT Act.

3.6 Children. The Site is not directed at collecting personal data from children under 18. Guardians should supervise minors’ use. If you believe a minor’s data was submitted, contact the Grievance Officer for deletion.

3.7 Cross-border processing. Hosting and CDN infrastructure may process technical and form-related data on servers outside India as part of standard global delivery. By using the Site you acknowledge this operational reality for a GitHub Pages deployment.

3.8 Retention. See the Data Retention Policy. In summary, personal data is kept only as long as needed for the purpose or legal hold, then deleted or access-restricted.

3.9 Automated decision-making. The Author does not use personal data submitted via the Site for automated decision-making that produces legal effects on the data principal.

4. User Rights & Responsibilities

Rights (DPDP Act, as applicable to this Site): you may request access to personal data you have provided, correction of inaccurate data, erasure of data no longer required for the purpose, and withdrawal of consent for future processing, by emailing the Grievance Officer with sufficient identity verification.

Responsibilities: provide accurate information; do not submit others’ personal data without authority; do not abuse forms; do not attempt intrusion, credential stuffing, or malware delivery. Zero Tolerance applies to spam and security abuse.

5. Content Protection Measures

The Author maintains a Zero Tolerance posture toward unauthorised copying, scraping, republication, commercial exploitation, and circumvention of technical protection measures. The following layered deterrence measures (soft DRM — not cryptographic DRM) are deployed on protected pages:

  • CSS / UI controls: user-select: none and related rules on protected views to restrict casual text selection outside expressly permitted fields.
  • Anti-copy / anti-cut / anti-paste: Client-side interception of copy, cut, paste, and context-menu actions on protected content (inputs, search, and [data-allow-copy] zones excepted).
  • Keyboard shortcut deterrence: Blocking of common shortcuts (including Ctrl/Cmd + C, X, A, S, U, P) outside editable fields.
  • Image protection: Drag-disable, protected image classes, and restricted touch callouts on media.
  • Watermark / print notice: Print and certain views may carry ownership notices identifying Bhargavsinh Sisodiya and the canonical site URL.
  • Soft DRM deterrence: Layered client-side controls intended to deter casual infringement. Circumvention for the purpose of infringement is Strictly Prohibited and may attract civil and criminal consequences under the Copyright Act, 1957 and the Information Technology Act, 2000, as applicable.

These measures are deterrents. They do not waive any statutory right of the Author. Fair dealing under the Copyright Act, 1957 remains subject to law and does not authorise wholesale reproduction, redistribution, or commercial use.

6. Penalties for Violation

Zero Tolerance applies to wilful infringement, data misuse, security circumvention, spam, and abusive conduct. Without prejudice to other remedies available in law or equity:

  • Immediate suspension, restriction, or blocking of access where technically feasible;
  • Civil claims for injunction, damages, delivery-up, and account of profits under the Copyright Act, 1957 and the Indian Contract Act, 1872;
  • Complaints and proceedings under the Information Technology Act, 2000 (including provisions relating to computer-related offences, damage to computer systems, and breach of protected systems, as applicable to the facts);
  • Reporting to competent law-enforcement or regulatory authorities where required or appropriate under Indian law.

Failure by the Author to enforce any provision in a particular instance shall not constitute a waiver of rights in that or any other instance. Severability: if any clause is held unenforceable, the remainder continues in force.
